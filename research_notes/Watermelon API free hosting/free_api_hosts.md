# Free hosting for the Watermelon AI Node.js/TypeScript API

Research snapshot: 2026-10-13. Sources are provider documentation or provider-owned policy/pricing pages unless a local project file is cited.

## Which platforms provide a genuine ongoing free tier versus trial credits?

### Takeaway

Cloudflare Workers, Deno Deploy, Vercel Hobby, Google Cloud Run, Oracle Cloud Always Free, Render Free, and Koyeb Free all have an ongoing free allowance or free instance as of the research date. Fly.io does not: its offer is only a short trial. None of the providers contractually promises the combination “always running, production-grade uptime, and free forever.”

### Cited Findings

- **Cloudflare Workers Free** is an ongoing default plan, not trial credit. It includes 100,000 requests per day and 10 ms of CPU time per invocation; internet egress is free. The paid Workers plan begins at US$5/month. — [Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)
- Cloudflare also provides ongoing free storage allowances: Workers KV includes 1 GB stored, 100,000 key reads/day, and 1,000 writes/day; D1 includes 5 GB total storage, 5 million rows read/day, and 100,000 rows written/day. Operations fail after a daily free limit is exhausted rather than silently becoming paid usage on the Free plan. — [Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)
- **Deno Deploy Free** is US$0/month and includes 1 million requests/month, 20 GiB egress/month, 10 active CPU hours/month, 150 GiB-hours of memory time, 10 apps, 10 GiB of revision storage, and Deno KV allowances of 1 GiB storage, 1 million 4-KiB read units/month, and 500,000 1-KiB write units/month. — [Deno Deploy pricing](https://deno.com/deploy/pricing)
- Deno explicitly pauses Free-plan applications until the next billing cycle if they exceed request, bandwidth, CPU, or memory limits. Deno also states that linking a credit card is required to unlock the full Free-plan limits; unverified organizations get restricted limits. — [Deno Deploy changelog](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/changelog/)
- **Vercel Hobby** is an ongoing free plan with 4 active CPU hours, 360 GB-hours of provisioned memory, and 1 million function invocations included. When a Hobby limit is exceeded, the affected feature generally cannot be used again until the rolling 30-day period resets. Hobby is restricted to personal, non-commercial use. — [Vercel Hobby plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)
- Vercel’s terms say Hobby is offered at Vercel’s sole discretion, may be changed or discontinued, and may be disabled without notice; therefore it is not a “free forever” commitment. — [Vercel terms, section 4](https://vercel.com/legal/terms)
- **Google Cloud Run Free Tier** is an ongoing monthly allowance, separate from the 90-day US$300 welcome-credit trial. For request-based billing it includes 2 million requests/month, 360,000 GB-seconds of memory, 180,000 vCPU-seconds, and 1 GB of North-America outbound transfer. Google says the Free Tier has no end date, but reserves the right to change or eliminate limits with 30 days’ notice. — [Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)
- Cloud Run requires an active Cloud Billing account. A paid billing account is charged for usage beyond the free allowance; a non-upgraded trial account is closed and its resources stopped when the trial ends. — [Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)
- **Oracle Cloud Always Free** resources are described as available for an unlimited period. Current compute allowance is up to two AMD E2.1.Micro instances, or 1,500 Ampere A1 OCPU-hours plus 9,000 GB-hours per month, which Oracle equates to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB RAM for an Always Free tenancy. — [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)
- Oracle requires a credit/debit card for identity verification and may place a temporary authorization hold. Always Free has no SLA or Oracle support. — [Oracle Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/)
- **Render Free** is ongoing free compute for previews/hobby use, but Render explicitly says not to use Free instances for production. A workspace gets 750 Free instance-hours/month, and exhausting them suspends all Free web services until the next month. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free)
- Render’s current Hobby workspace has 5 GB outbound bandwidth/month; excess is US$0.15/GB if a payment method is present, while a workspace without a payment method is suspended for the rest of the month. — [Render outbound bandwidth](https://render.com/docs/outbound-bandwidth); [Render pricing](https://render.com/pricing)
- **Koyeb Free** provides one free web service per organization with 0.1 vCPU, 512 MB RAM, and 2 GB SSD. Koyeb describes this as a “forever free tier,” but also says the Free instance is for preview/hobby work and should not be used for production. — [Koyeb instances](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/reference/instances); [Koyeb pricing FAQ](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/faqs/pricing)
- Koyeb’s current billing FAQ says it requires a credit card, places and cancels a US$29 preauthorization hold, and charges the prorated selected Pro plan at signup; the account can then be downgraded to Starter. One free instance itself is never billed. — [Koyeb pricing FAQ](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/faqs/pricing)
- **Fly.io** has no ongoing free compute tier for new users. Its trial is 2 total VM hours or 7 days, whichever comes first; trial VMs stop after 5 minutes, and applications stop when the trial ends until a payment method is added. — [Fly.io Free Trial](https://fly.io/docs/about/free-trial/)
- After the Fly.io trial, organizations require a credit card and provisioned resources are usage-billed. — [Fly.io pricing](https://fly.io/docs/about/pricing/)

### Inferences

- “Genuine ongoing free tier” means the allowance renews or remains available without relying on one-time trial credits. By that definition, Cloudflare, Deno, Vercel, Cloud Run, Oracle, Render, and Koyeb qualify, but their terms still allow limits and products to change. — [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/); [Deno pricing](https://deno.com/deploy/pricing); [Vercel terms](https://vercel.com/legal/terms); [Google Free Tier terms](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features); [Oracle FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/); [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Koyeb pricing FAQ](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/faqs/pricing)
- “Free forever” is marketing shorthand, not an uptime or price contract. Oracle comes closest on duration (“unlimited period”), while Google says “no end date,” but both retain important operational caveats and no free-tier SLA. — [Oracle FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/); [Google Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)

### Gaps

- Cloudflare’s current official documentation confirms that Free is the default plan but does not clearly state on the cited pages whether every new Free account can deploy without any card verification.
- Vercel’s current official plan pages do not clearly state whether a card is required at initial Hobby signup. The plan itself cannot incur function overages: usage is paused/blocked when limits are reached. — [Vercel Hobby plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)
- Render says services without a payment method are suspended rather than billed, which implies a card is optional for some accounts; Render may still request card details for abuse verification. The exact trigger is not publicly specified. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Render terms](https://render.com/terms)

## Which options can host an Express-like Node API with 36 endpoints?

### Takeaway

Cloud Run, Oracle VM, Render, Koyeb, Fly.io, and modern Vercel Functions can execute the Express API with relatively little routing work. Deno Deploy can run most Node/Express code but changes the runtime and deployment model. Cloudflare now supports Express, but the present Watermelon API’s disk-based database/uploads, 50 MB JSON bodies, and compute-heavy authentication/image paths make a straight Workers deployment unsafe without a storage and architecture migration.

### Cited Findings

- The project starts `tsx server.ts`, uses Express 4, and includes Firestore and Node-focused dependencies. — [Local package.json](../../package.json)
- The current storage layer reads and rewrites `data/watermelon_db.json`, appends an audit log, and writes uploads to a local directory. — [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
- The API configures `express.json` and URL-encoded request limits to 50 MB, and invokes a separate FastAPI vision service through `VISION_SERVICE_URL`. — [Local server.ts](../../server.ts)
- **Cloud Run:** accepts arbitrary containers, so an ordinary Node/Express process listening on `$PORT` is a natural fit. Its container filesystem is writable but in-memory and is lost when an instance stops, so the JSON database and uploads cannot remain on local disk. — [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract)
- **Vercel:** officially supports existing Express apps with zero-configuration detection; the whole Express app becomes one Vercel Function. Full Node.js APIs are supported. — [Express on Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/frameworks/backend/express); [Vercel Node.js runtime](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/node-js)
- Vercel Functions impose a 4.5 MB request/response payload limit, while this project accepts image/base64 JSON up to 50 MB. Vercel recommends direct-to-blob uploads for larger payloads. — [Vercel Function limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations); [Vercel large-payload guidance](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/how-to-bypass-vercel-body-size-limit-serverless-functions); [Local server.ts](../../server.ts)
- **Cloudflare Workers:** Cloudflare now documents running Express with Node compatibility enabled, and its Node HTTP server APIs are supported for modern compatibility dates. — [Cloudflare Express tutorial](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/tutorials/deploy-an-express-app/); [Cloudflare Node HTTP API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/http/)
- Cloudflare’s filesystem is a per-request in-memory VFS: `/bundle` is read-only and `/tmp` disappears after each request. Free Workers also have a 10 ms CPU limit per invocation and 128 MB memory, despite allowing request bodies up to 100 MB on a Cloudflare Free account. — [Cloudflare filesystem](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/fs/); [Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)
- **Deno Deploy:** Deno says most Node code runs without modification, provides npm and Express compatibility, and the new Deploy runtime supports filesystem access, network requests, subprocesses, FFI, and Node native addons. — [Deno Node/npm compatibility](https://docs.deno.com/runtime/fundamentals/node/); [Deno Deploy runtime](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/reference/runtime/)
- Deno Deploy is serverless: instances may stop after 5 seconds to 10 minutes of no traffic, multiple isolated instances can run concurrently, and they do not share disk. Durable application state therefore belongs in Deno KV or an external database, not the current local JSON file. — [Deno Deploy runtime](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/reference/runtime/); [Deno KV](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/kv/)
- **Render and Koyeb:** both run container/native web services and can host an ordinary Node listener; however, their free local disks are not durable. Render explicitly loses all local files on restart/redeploy/spin-down, and Koyeb Free cannot attach a persistent Volume. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Koyeb instances](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/reference/instances)
- **Oracle Always Free VM:** provides a conventional VM plus Always Free block volume, so it can preserve the JSON database and uploaded files and run Node under systemd/Docker with the fewest code changes. Always Free includes 200 GB combined boot/block storage. — [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)
- **Fly.io:** a Machine and volume can host the API, but it becomes paid after the short trial, so it does not answer a continuing-free requirement. — [Fly.io Free Trial](https://fly.io/docs/about/free-trial/); [Fly.io pricing](https://fly.io/docs/about/pricing/)

### Inferences

- Endpoint count itself is not a meaningful blocker: 36 Express routes can live in one container or one Express function. The real blockers are state durability, large image payloads, CPU/memory, and the separate FastAPI service. — [Express on Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/frameworks/backend/express); [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract); [Local server.ts](../../server.ts)
- Oracle VM is the easiest “lift and shift” for the current disk-backed architecture, but Cloud Run is the cleaner managed target after moving `watermelon_db.json`/uploads to Firestore plus object storage. — [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm); [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract); [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
- Cloudflare Workers is technically able to host Express in 2026, but Watermelon AI would need a material migration to D1/KV/R2, and the 10 ms free CPU cap plus 128 MB memory make base64 image processing and password hashing a poor fit for the Free plan. — [Cloudflare Express tutorial](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/tutorials/deploy-an-express-app/); [Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Cloudflare filesystem](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/fs/)
- Vercel is unsuitable for the current image endpoints until the client uploads images directly to object storage or caps payloads below 4.5 MB. — [Vercel Function limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations); [Local server.ts](../../server.ts)

### Gaps

- This research did not benchmark cold-start latency, bundle size, or memory consumption of the actual Watermelon API on any provider.
- It did not validate whether every dependency (especially AI SDKs and any transitive native modules) works under Deno Deploy or Cloudflare Workers; those need a deployment proof-of-concept.
- The FastAPI vision service has separate Python/model/GPU/CPU requirements and is not covered by the Node-only free quota comparison.

## Which platforms sleep when idle, can exceed free quota, or impose network/storage limits?

### Takeaway

Render and Koyeb have visible, long wake-up delays. Cloud Run, Vercel, and Deno scale to zero but wake automatically; their endpoints remain routable, though the first request can be slower. Oracle can keep a VM process running but may reclaim an idle Always Free instance. Cloudflare uses isolates rather than a sleeping VM, but strict per-request limits can reject work. Free quotas either fail/pause service or become billable depending on whether a paid billing method is enabled.

- Render’s approximately one-minute wake delay is too disruptive for an interactive agricultural assistant; Koyeb’s 1–5 second wake is less severe but still visible. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Koyeb scale to zero](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/run-and-scale/scale-to-zero)
- A keep-alive ping is not a sound free-tier strategy: it defeats providers’ intended scale-to-zero behavior, consumes quotas, may breach fair-use expectations, and still does not create an uptime SLA. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Koyeb scale to zero](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/run-and-scale/scale-to-zero); [Cloud Run minimum instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/min-instances)

### Gaps

- Providers generally do not publish guaranteed cold-start latency for a real application of Watermelon AI’s size; Deno and Koyeb publish indicative values, not an application-specific SLA.
- Exact Thai-user round-trip latency was not measured.
- The current Cloudflare documentation does not describe Workers Free as “sleeping” or “always on”; it documents isolate/resource behavior instead, so no zero-cold-start guarantee should be inferred.

## What are the key operational and vendor-lock-in tradeoffs?

### Takeaway

Containers/VMs minimize runtime lock-in; proprietary edge storage and function conventions increase it. Moving persistence out of local files is unavoidable for horizontally scaled or scale-to-zero managed hosting and is also the most important reliability improvement for Watermelon AI.

### Cited Findings

- Cloud Run’s portable container model preserves the Node runtime and Express entrypoint, but its ephemeral filesystem forces migration to an external database/object store. — [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract)
- Oracle VM preserves a normal Linux process and persistent block disk, reducing application changes, but transfers patching, firewalling, TLS/reverse proxy, monitoring, backups, and recovery to the operator; Oracle provides no SLA/support for Always Free. — [Oracle Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/); [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)
- Vercel provides the simplest Express conversion because the app becomes one function, but its 4.5 MB payload ceiling and Hobby non-commercial restriction are hard constraints for this project. — [Express on Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/frameworks/backend/express); [Vercel Function limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations); [Vercel Hobby plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)
- Cloudflare’s D1/KV/R2 bindings and 10 ms Free CPU model are materially different from a disk-backed Node server, creating the greatest rewrite and platform coupling among the compared options. — [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/); [Cloudflare filesystem](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/fs/)
- Deno has strong Node/npm compatibility but changes the production runtime and deploy geography; the current platform offers only US/EU/global, and applications may be started/stopped at any time. — [Deno Node compatibility](https://docs.deno.com/runtime/fundamentals/node/); [Deno Deploy CLI regions](https://docs.deno.com/runtime/reference/cli/deploy/); [Deno Deploy runtime](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/reference/runtime/)
- Render and Koyeb have low deployment friction for Node services, but their own docs classify free instances as preview/hobby rather than production and deny durable attached disk on Free. — [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free); [Koyeb instances](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/reference/instances)

### Inferences

- The local JSON database’s read-modify-write model is not safe for multiple concurrent instances even if a provider offered persistent shared disk; moving structured state to Firestore/Postgres is a correctness requirement before autoscaling. — [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts); [Cloud Run runtime contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract); [Deno runtime](https://docs.deno.com/deploy/reference/runtime/)
- The project already depends on `@google-cloud/firestore`, so Cloud Run + Firestore has lower incremental vendor/runtime risk than a complete Cloudflare D1/KV rewrite. — [Local package.json](../../package.json); [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract)
- Direct object-storage uploads using signed URLs would reduce API memory, avoid Vercel’s payload limit pattern, and make images durable independently of API instances. — [Vercel large-payload guidance](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/how-to-bypass-vercel-body-size-limit-serverless-functions); [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
- Oracle’s apparent cost advantage comes with the highest operational burden and a non-obvious idle-reclamation risk, so it is appropriate for a technically managed prototype but not the strongest unattended production choice. — [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm); [Oracle Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/)

### Gaps

- No provider-specific data-processing agreement, Thai PDPA posture, encryption-key design, or backup/restore test was evaluated.
- No cost model was produced for the separate vision service, Gemini calls, Firestore, or object storage; these can dominate the hosting cost even if the Node API compute remains free.

## What is the strongest recommendation for Watermelon AI’s Node API?

### Takeaway

Use **Google Cloud Run in Singapore (`asia-southeast1`) with scale-to-zero (`min instances = 0`)**, after moving the JSON database/audit trail to Firestore or another managed database and uploads to object storage. It is the best balance of current-code compatibility, Thai latency, operational safety, and a continuing free allowance. It is “available on demand,” not literally always warm; if zero cold starts or production uptime is required, budget for at least one warm instance and a paid support/SLA posture.

For a zero-refactor demonstration only, Oracle Always Free VM is the fallback because it can preserve the current disk-backed design and keep a normal Node process running, but idle reclamation, no SLA, and self-management make it weaker for production.

### Cited Findings

- Cloud Run can run the existing Node/Express server in a container and provides 2 million requests/month plus compute/memory allowances under request-based Free Tier billing. — [Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features); [Local package.json](../../package.json)
- Singapore is an available Cloud Run region and is geographically appropriate for Thai users. — [Cloud Run locations](https://cloud.google.com/run/docs/locations)
- With `min instances = 0`, Cloud Run scales to zero; setting a warm minimum incurs charges and still does not guarantee an instance can never restart. — [Cloud Run autoscaling](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/about-instance-autoscaling); [Cloud Run minimum instances](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/configuring/min-instances)
- Cloud Run’s local filesystem is explicitly non-persistent, while Watermelon AI currently stores its principal JSON data, audit log, and uploaded media on disk. — [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract); [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
- Google Cloud requires billing and bills overages beyond the Free Tier, so budgets/alerts and conservative max-instance limits are required; Google’s Free Tier itself has no end date but may change with notice. — [Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)
- Oracle’s VM allowance and 200 GB block storage make it the closest match to the current architecture, but Oracle can reclaim idle instances and Free Tier has no SLA. — [Oracle Always Free resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm); [Oracle Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/)

### Inferences

- Recommended rollout order:
  1. Replace `PersistentDatabase` JSON writes and audit-file appends with a transactional managed database; retain explicit error reporting rather than falling back to empty state. — [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
  2. Move uploads to object storage and keep signed URL behavior; do not rely on container disk. — [Cloud Run container contract](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/container-contract); [Local server-storage.ts](../../server-storage.ts)
  3. Containerize the Node API, deploy to Cloud Run Singapore with `min=0`, set a conservative maximum instance count, enable billing budgets/alerts, and test the largest image route plus authentication/OTP flows. — [Cloud Run locations](https://cloud.google.com/run/docs/locations); [Google Cloud Free Program](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)
  4. Deploy the FastAPI vision service separately; do not assume the Node API’s free allowance covers model inference. — [Local server.ts](../../server.ts)
- If “no card and absolutely zero chance of charges” is a hard requirement, choose Cloudflare Workers only after a significant D1/R2 redesign, or Render for a temporary demo. Neither meets the current architecture plus production-like 24/7 requirement without unacceptable constraints. — [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/); [Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Render free instances](https://render.com/docs/free)
